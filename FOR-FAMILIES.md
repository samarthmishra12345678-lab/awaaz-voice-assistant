# For Families & Caregivers / परिवार के लिए / कुटुंबासाठी

> Awaaz is used by someone else's parent. This page is for the person setting it up — and for the person who gets the call when a dose is missed.

---

## 🎯 Who this is for

- A son or daughter setting Awaaz up on a parent's phone
- A caregiver who wants to be reachable if a reminder goes unanswered
- Anyone helping an elderly or low-literacy relative use a phone

---

## 🛠️ Setting it up for someone else — 10 minutes

### 1. Install it on their home screen (2 min)

Open Awaaz in **Chrome** (Android) or **Safari** (iPhone), then choose *Add to Home Screen*. Rename the shortcut to something they will recognise:

- **"Dawai"** (medicine)
- **"Bol ke batao"**
- **"Awaaz"**

> Do not leave it buried in a browser tab. An elderly user will not find a bookmark.

### 2. Grant the permissions (1 min)

Open the app once yourself and tap through:
- **Microphone → Allow**
- **Notifications → Allow** (when you set the first test reminder)

Do this *with* them, not for them, if they are able — the first successful reminder should be theirs.

### 3. Save yourself as the caregiver (1 min)

Say or type: **"caregiver number <your number>"**

This is the single most important step. Without it, the missed-dose escalation has nowhere to go.

### 4. Set one real reminder together (2 min)

Have them say it themselves:

> **"Roz subah 8 baje dawai yaad dilao"**

Let them hear it speak the confirmation back. One success is worth ten explanations.

### 5. Save an emergency number (1 min)

Say: **"SOS number <number>"** — ideally a person who is geographically close, not the same person who lives abroad.

### 6. Set the font size and theme for their eyes (1 min)

Say **"Text bada karo"** a few times, and **"Dark mode karo"** if they use it at night. These settings persist.

### 7. Test the escalation once (2 min)

Set a reminder 4 minutes ahead. Do not confirm it. Watch the escalation banner appear with your number on it.

> Do this once while you are there, so you both know what it looks like before it ever matters.

---

## 📞 The call you might get

If a reminder goes unanswered for three minutes, the app offers to:

- **Call** the caregiver number
- **WhatsApp** a prefilled message: *"Dawai confirm nahi hui…"*

**What it means:** a reminder fired and nobody tapped "Theek hai". It does **not** prove the medicine was not taken — it proves nobody confirmed it. It is a nudge to check in, not an alarm to panic.

**What it does not mean:** it is not a medical alert, it does not detect falls, and it does not contact emergency services.

---

## ⚠️ What Awaaz does NOT do

Say this out loud, because families assume too much of apps:

| It does not | Because |
|---|---|
| Work while the app is fully closed and the phone is locked | True background push needs a push server or a native app — on the roadmap |
| Detect falls or health events | It is a voice assistant, not a medical device |
| Replace a caregiver | It reduces the number of things a caregiver has to remember |
| Sync between phones | Everything stays on one device, by design (see [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md)) |
| Guarantee speech accuracy | Accents and noisy rooms affect it — hence the typing fallback |

> **The honest version:** Awaaz lowers the effort of asking for help from "find the app, unlock it, navigate, type" to "press once and speak". It does not make an elderly person independent. It makes them *less dependent on being near you at the exact moment they need something*.

## ✅ A checklist you can hand over

- [ ] Added to home screen, renamed to something recognisable
- [ ] Microphone permission allowed
- [ ] Notification permission allowed
- [ ] Caregiver number saved
- [ ] Emergency (SOS) number saved
- [ ] One daily medicine reminder set *by the user*
- [ ] Font size set for their eyes
- [ ] Escalation tested once, together
- [ ] Family knows: a missed-dose alert is a nudge, not an emergency
